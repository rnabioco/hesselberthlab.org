abstract: During development in the thymus, invariant natural killer T (iNKT) cells
  commit to one of three major functionally different subsets, iNKT1, iNKT2, and iNKT17.
  Here, we show that T cell antigen receptor (TCR) signal strength governs the development
  of iNKT cell subsets, with strong signaling promoting iNKT2 and iNKT17 development.
  Altering TCR diversity or signaling diminishes iNKT2 and iNKT17 cell subset development
  in a cell-intrinsic manner. Decreased TCR signaling affects the persistence of Egr2
  expression and the upregulation of PLZF. By genome-wide comparison of chromatin
  accessibility, we identify a subset of iNKT2-specific regulatory elements containing
  NFAT and Egr binding motifs that is less accessible in iNKT2 cells that develop
  from reduced TCR signaling. These data suggest that variable TCR signaling modulates
  regulatory element activity at NFAT and Egr binding sites exerting a determinative
  influence on the dynamics of gene enhancer accessibility and the developmental fate
  of iNKT cells.
