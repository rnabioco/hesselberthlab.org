abstract: "Ligation by plant and fungal RNA ligases yields an internal 2'-phosphate
  group on each RNA ligation product. In budding yeast, this covalent mark occurs
  at the splice junction of two targets of ligation: intron-containing tRNAs and the
  messenger RNA HAC1 The repertoire of RNA molecules repaired by RNA ligation has
  not been explored due to a lack of unbiased approaches for identifying RNA ligation
  products. Here, we define several unique signals produced by 2'-phosphorylated RNAs
  during nanopore sequencing. A 2'-phosphate at the splice junction of HAC1 mRNA inhibits
  5' $i̊ghtarrow$ 3' degradation, enabling detection of decay intermediates in yeast
  RNA repair mutants by nanopore sequencing. During direct RNA sequencing, intact
  2'-phosphorylated RNAs on HAC1 and tRNAs produce diagnostic changes in nanopore
  current properties and base calling features, including stalls produced as the modified
  RNA translocates through the nanopore motor protein. These approaches enable directed
  studies to identify novel RNA repair events in other contexts."
